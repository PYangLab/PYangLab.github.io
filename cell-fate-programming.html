Research narrative hub

Computational blueprints for cell-fate programming

We develop interpretable computational systems biology methods that turn single-cell, spatial and multimodal omics into testable models of cell identity, cell-fate programming and disease-state intervention.

Cell-fate programming aims to guide cells from one identity or state to another, with applications in disease modelling, drug discovery and regenerative medicine. A central challenge is deciding which regulators, pathways or candidate compounds are most likely to support a desired transition. Our work addresses this challenge by connecting statistical modelling, interpretable AI and omics technologies to experimentally testable hypotheses.

Programme focus

  • Model cell identity and cell-state transitions.
  • Infer regulatory programs from omics data.
  • Prioritise candidate regulators and compounds.
  • Benchmark engineered states for fidelity and function.

The synthesis

The Stem Cell Reports article Computational blueprints for cell fate programming provides a conceptual roadmap for this programme. It frames computational methods as part of an iterative design-test-learn cycle: models nominate regulators or interventions, experiments test the predicted transitions, and new data refine the next round of modelling.

Design-test-learn cycle

From cell-state measurement to candidate intervention

1

Measure

Profile cells using single-cell, spatial, multimodal, proteomic or phosphoproteomic data.

2

Model

Learn cell identities, trajectories, regulatory programs and disease-associated states.

3

Prioritise

Rank candidate regulators, pathways, signalling events and compounds for testing.

4

Test

Evaluate interventions in stem-cell, organoid, perturbation or disease-relevant systems.

5

Learn

Benchmark engineered states and update models using new experimental evidence.

Why this matters now

The field is moving from descriptive cell atlases toward predictive and perturbation-aware models. Single-cell and spatial omics, multimodal measurements, perturbation screens and deep learning expand what can be predicted, but they also introduce challenges in reproducibility, domain shift and interpretability.

Our programme focuses on the practical middle ground: computational models that are powerful enough to nominate cell-fate interventions, but interpretable and benchmarked enough to support experimental decision-making.

Programme statement

By integrating interpretable AI, statistical modelling and omics technologies with stem-cell, organoid and disease biology, we aim to build reusable computational blueprints for modelling cell identity, prioritising candidate interventions and improving the design of cell-fate programming experiments.
